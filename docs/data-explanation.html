Data Explanation

About Risk Scores

Our risk scores are comprehensive assessment metrics calculated from VAERS (Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System) data, ranging from 0 to 10.

Calculation Method

Base Score = Mortality Rate × 2.0 + Severe Reaction Rate × 0.5

Then adjusted for personal factors:
  • Age 65+: +1.5
  • Age 50-64: +1.0
  • Diabetes: +0.8
  • Heart Disease: +0.8
  • Immunodeficiency: +1.0
  • Previous COVID Infection: -0.3

Risk Levels

  • Low Risk Score < 4.0: Relatively few adverse reaction reports
  • Medium Risk Score 4.0-7.0: Some severe reactions reported
  • High Risk Score ≥ 7.0: Higher proportion of severe reaction reports

About Symptom Statistics

Symptom data comes from adverse event reports submitted by vaccinated individuals, showing the frequency of different symptoms after vaccination.

Severity Classification

  • ⚠️ Severe Symptoms: Symptoms that may lead to hospitalization, disability, or death, such as difficulty breathing, chest pain, blood clots
  • 🟡 Common Symptoms: Mild to moderate symptoms experienced by most vaccinated individuals, such as headache, fatigue, fever
  • 🟢 Mild Symptoms: Less frequent or mild symptoms that usually don't require special treatment

Time Distribution

The symptom time distribution shows the number of symptom reports in different time periods after vaccination, helping understand when symptoms typically occur.

Data Sources

VAERS (Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System)
The US vaccine adverse event reporting system, operated by CDC and FDA.

Data URL: https://vaers.hhs.gov/data/datasets.html

Important Disclaimer

⚠️ Disclaimer

  • VAERS is a passive reporting system and may have underreporting and reporting biases
  • Correlation does not imply causation - reported events may not be caused by vaccines
  • This platform data is for reference only and cannot replace medical advice
  • Please consult a healthcare professional for any health concerns

How to Interpret the Data

More Reports ≠ More Dangerous

High report volume may indicate:

  • More people received that batch
  • Higher public awareness
  • Stronger reporting practices by healthcare providers

Recommendations

  • Consider both risk scores and symptom lists together
  • Pay attention to severe symptom rates, not just absolute numbers
  • Consult your doctor if you have underlying health conditions
